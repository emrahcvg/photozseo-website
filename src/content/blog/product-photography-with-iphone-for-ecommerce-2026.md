Yes, you can take marketplace-ready product photos with an iPhone. You need soft light from one direction, a plain backdrop, a steady phone, and the same set of angles for every product. The camera is rarely the limit. Inconsistent light, cluttered backgrounds and random framing are what make listings look amateur. This guide is a practical, low-cost setup you can repeat for a whole catalog.

*Last verified: November 2026. Sources: Apple Support ("Change camera formats", "Use iPhone camera tools"), Etsy Help Center ("How to Add Photos to Your Listings"), Shopify Help Center ("Product media").*

## What gear do you actually need?

| Item | Why | Rough alternative |
|---|---|---|
| A recent iPhone | Sharp, consistent output | Any phone with a clean lens |
| Phone tripod or stack of books | Same framing every shot, no blur | Lean the phone on a mug |
| Window or softbox | Soft, even light | Overcast daylight near a window |
| White card or foam board | Bounce light into shadows | A white sheet or paper |
| Seamless backdrop | Clean, distraction-free background | White poster board curved into a sweep |
| Microfiber cloth | Fingerprints on the lens soften every shot | Clean T-shirt |

Wipe the lens first. It is the cheapest quality upgrade there is.

## How should you light a product?

Use one large soft source. Put the product beside a window, turn off overhead lights so colors do not mix, and place a white card on the opposite side to open up shadows. Direct sunlight creates hard shadows and glare on glossy items, so diffuse it with a thin white curtain or paper.

Keep the same light position for the whole batch. Consistent shadows and brightness make a catalog look professional, even if the individual photos are simple.

## What background works best?

A plain, uncluttered surface. For the main image, many marketplaces want a clean, often white, background, so check the platform's current rules. Our guide to [white background product photos](/blog/white-background-product-photos) covers this in detail. If you shoot on a messy background, you can remove it afterward; see [removing backgrounds on iPhone](/blog/remove-background-product-photos-iphone).

Curve the backdrop upward behind the product so there is no visible horizon line. Keep the product a few centimeters away from the backdrop to soften shadows.

## Which angles should you shoot?

Use the same checklist for every product. Buyers cannot touch the item, so each shot answers a question.

| Shot | What it answers |
|---|---|
| Front | What does it look like? Your main image |
| Back | Is there a different design, pocket, port or label on the back? |
| Side | How thick or deep is it? |
| Detail (close-up) | Texture, stitching, finish, quality |
| Top | Shape from above, layout of parts |
| Label | Brand, size, ingredients, model number, certifications |
| Lifestyle | Scale and use in context, with a hand, room or model |

Shoot the lifestyle image last, and do not use it as the main image where a plain background is expected.

## Which camera settings matter?

- **Grid.** Turn on the Camera grid in settings to keep horizons and product edges straight, and to center the item consistently.
- **Lock exposure and focus.** Apple's guide to iPhone camera tools says to touch and hold the focus area until AE/AF Lock appears. Tapping the screen unlocks. This stops brightness shifting between shots.
- **Do not zoom digitally.** Move the phone or tripod instead. If you want a tighter frame, use the lens options on your model, or crop later from a high-resolution original.
- **Same distance, same height.** Mark the tripod position with tape.
- **HEIC vs. JPEG.** Apple says Settings, Camera, Formats, Most Compatible saves photos as JPEG, and High Efficiency uses the space-saving HEIF/HEVC formats. HEIC is accepted by Etsy and Shopify per their help pages, but JPEG avoids compatibility surprises elsewhere. Choose one and stay consistent.
- **Shoot at full resolution.** You can shrink later; you cannot recover detail you never captured.

## How should you edit and export?

1. **Back up originals.** Never edit the only copy.
2. **Straighten and crop** to a consistent framing. For long products, pad to a square canvas instead of cutting the edges off.
3. **Light adjustments only.** Slightly raise exposure or shadows, neutralize color cast, and check that the product color matches reality. Wrong colors cause returns.
4. **Resize and compress copies per marketplace.** Shopify says 2048 x 2048 px usually displays best, Etsy recommends at least 2000 px, and Amazon prefers larger than 1,000 px on the longest side. See [how to resize for multiple marketplaces](/blog/resize-product-photos-multiple-marketplaces) and [WebP vs. JPEG vs. PNG](/blog/webp-jpeg-png-product-photos-2026).
5. **Rename files** descriptively before upload; see [how to name product photos for SEO](/blog/how-to-name-product-photos-for-seo).
