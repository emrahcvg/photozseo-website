**Yes, you can use supplier photos in a dropshipping store, but only when you have the right to.** Photographs are protected by copyright automatically, so an image that is easy to download is not automatically free to use. The reliable path is to get permission or a licence, check image quality, then process each file for your channel with a consistent size, background and name.

*Last verified: November 2026. Sources: U.S. Copyright Office, General Questions FAQ ("When is my work protected?"); Shopify Help Center, "Product media types"; Google Merchant Center Help, "Image [image_link]". This is general information, not legal advice; ask a qualified lawyer about specific cases and about the laws in your country.*

## Who owns the supplier's photos?

Usually the person who took them, or the company that paid for them. The U.S. Copyright Office FAQ says a work is "under copyright protection the moment it is created and fixed in a tangible form", lists photographs as protected works, and says registration is voluntary. So the lack of a watermark or a copyright notice tells you nothing about permission. Laws vary by country, but the principle of automatic protection is widely shared.

## How do I check I can use an image?

| Source of image | What to do |
|---|---|
| Supplier's reseller or media kit | Read the terms. Look for wording that allows resellers to use images on their own store. Save a copy. |
| Marketplace listing (AliExpress, Alibaba and similar) | The photo usually belongs to the seller. Message them and ask for written permission or a licensed set. |
| Manufacturer or brand site | Check the brand's press or partner page for a media licence. |
| Stock photo or licensed library | Confirm the licence covers commercial e-commerce use. |
| Your own photos or sample orders | You hold the rights. Order a sample and photograph it. |

If you cannot get a clear yes, do not use the image. Taking your own photos, even simple ones on a plain background, is the lowest-risk option, and it is the one that sets your store apart.

## How do I check supplier image quality?

Before spending time editing, review each image:

- Is it large enough? Google Merchant Center requires at least 500 x 500 pixels, and recommends about 1500 x 1500 or larger.
- Is it sharp, well lit and in colour that matches the real product?
- Does it contain a supplier watermark, logo, Chinese or other overlay text, or a price badge? Google lists watermarks, promotional overlays and added logos as prohibited in Merchant Center images, so those need to go.
- Does it show items that are not included in the order? Images should show what the customer receives.

## What is the process from supplier image to compliant listing?

1. **Confirm rights.** Get the permission in writing.
2. **Select images.** Keep the clearest main image and several angles.
3. **Resize for your channel.** Shopify supports up to 5000 x 5000 px and under 20 MB, and recommends 2048 x 2048 px for square product images. Other channels have different limits; see [marketplace image sizes](/marketplace-image-sizes).
4. **Clean the background.** A consistent white or neutral background looks more trustworthy. See [white background product photos](/blog/white-background-product-photos).
5. **Compress and choose a format.** Smaller files load faster. See [WebP, JPEG or PNG](/blog/webp-jpeg-png-product-photos-2026).
6. **Rename files.** Use a descriptive name instead of `1005001234567.jpg`. See [how to name product photos for SEO](/blog/how-to-name-product-photos-for-seo).
7. **Write alt text and title yourself.** Describe your own product in your own words; do not copy supplier text. See [alt text examples](/blog/product-image-alt-text-examples).
8. **Test the result.** Check the listing on mobile.

## Why do duplicate images matter?

When dozens of stores use exactly the same supplier photo and text, a shopper (or a reverse image search) can find the same product elsewhere, and your listing offers nothing that distinguishes it. Search engines also tend to show only one or a few of many near-identical results. You reduce this by using original or licensed photos, adding your own lifestyle shots or sample photos, and writing original descriptions.
