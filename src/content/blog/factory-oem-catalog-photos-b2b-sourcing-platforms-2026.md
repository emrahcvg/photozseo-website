**An OEM or ODM factory should shoot its catalog for one reader: a buyer who is comparing five suppliers on a screen and cannot pick anything up.** That means a clean full-product image first, then close-ups that answer the questions a buyer would otherwise ask by email: what is it made of, how is it joined, what does the label say, how is it packed. The photos are only half of the job. The other half is how consistently you name, size and reuse them on sourcing platforms, in RFQ replies and in the quote PDF.

## What does a buyer actually look for in a factory catalog?

Importers and brand owners who source OEM products tend to scan for proof of three things: that the product matches their spec, that the factory is consistent, and that someone there answers properly. Photos support the first two.

Consider a hypothetical factory making 60 stainless-steel kitchen products. The buyer wants to know the gauge of the metal, how the handle is fixed, whether the finish is brushed or polished, and what the carton looks like. A single hero photo per product answers none of that. A set of six does.

| Image slot | What it shows | Question it answers |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Main | Whole product, plain background, centred | What is it? |
| 2. Angle | Top or side, same lighting | What is the shape and depth? |
| 3. Material | Close-up of surface or cut edge | What finish and material? |
| 4. Construction | Joint, weld, stitch, hinge, fastener | How well is it made? |
| 5. Label / branding | Logo area, hang tag, nameplate | Can my brand go here? |
| 6. Packaging | Inner pack and export carton | How does it ship? |

Keep the order identical across all 60 products. A buyer scrolling the catalog learns the pattern in two products and then reads faster.

## What do B2B sourcing platforms say about product images?

Rules differ by platform and are edited over time, so treat any number you read in a blog as a starting point, not a requirement.

| Platform | What the official page says (as verified) | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| HKTDC Sourcing | Photography Tips: plain white background; separate photos for top, bottom and sides (up to 15 photos plus a video); item fills roughly 80% to 90% of the frame; no email addresses or URLs in photos; JPG, JPEG or PNG, larger than 550 x 550 px with 1,500 x 1,500 px recommended, above 72 dpi, under 5 MB, RGB | Use these as a conservative baseline and re-check the live page |
| Global Sources | Supplier Image Guidelines historically ask for a white background, with an exception for partly transparent products. Older versions list small pixel sizes and conflicting resolution values | Verify current numbers inside your supplier account |
| Alibaba.com | Specs sit in seller-account help; could not confirm from public pages | Read the upload screen and help centre in your account |
| Made-in-China.com | Public help centre did not list image specs | Ask your account manager or read the upload screen |

The practical lesson: export from a large master (for example, the longest side at 2,000 px or more if your camera allows it) and downscale per platform. Upscaling a small file later never works.

## How should a factory shoot the products?

You do not need a studio. You need repeatability.

1. **One fixed shooting corner.** Same table, same backdrop, same light. A white sweep of paper, two diffused lights and a tripod will beat a different setup each week.
2. **Shoot by batch, not by order.** Photograph all samples of one collection in one session so colour and shadow match.
3. **Add scale.** Put a ruler or a hand in one extra frame. Buyers often ask for dimensions that you have already measured.
4. **Show defects honestly.** If a casting has a parting line, show it. A buyer who finds it on arrival is a lost customer; a buyer who saw it and approved it is a repeat order.
5. **Mark mock-ups.** If you show a buyer's logo on a product that does not exist yet, label the image "mock-up".
6. **Shoot packaging last.** Carton marks, barcodes and pallet photos help the logistics person on the buyer's side.

## How do RFQs change the photo job?

An RFQ (request for quotation) arrives with a quantity, a spec and sometimes a target price. Your reply competes against others, usually within a day or two. The fastest replies reuse an organised photo library.

A realistic hypothetical flow for a factory with 60 SKUs:

| Step | Action | Photo asset used |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Match the RFQ to a SKU or a near variant | Catalog main + angle images |
| 2 | Confirm customisation (colour, logo, size) | Variant and mock-up images |
| 3 | Offer a sample | Sample photo set (see below) |
| 4 | Send the quote | PDF with thumbnail per line item |
| 5 | Follow up after the sample ships | Packed-box photo and tracking |

## What belongs in a sample photo set?

A sample is a promise. Photograph it before it leaves:

- front, back and both sides, with a ruler visible
- the label or nameplate, legible
- any detail the buyer flagged in the RFQ (hinge, zipper, seam, thread)
- the sample in its packaging, sealed, with the shipping label visible
- a short clip if the product has a mechanism

Store these under the RFQ or sample number. If the buyer later says "the sample was different", you have dated evidence either way.

## What goes into the quote PDF?

Buyers expect a quote they can forward to their finance and logistics colleagues. Typical fields are the product line with a thumbnail, quantity, unit price, the price basis (for example an Incoterms 2020 term with a named place), payment terms, MOQ, lead time, validity date and your bank details. HS codes help the buyer estimate duty. Whether VAT, export rebates or specific documents apply depends on the countries involved, so confirm with your own accountant or customs broker rather than copying a template.

## A pre-publish checklist for factory catalog photos

- [ ] Main image shows the whole product on a plain background
- [ ] Same slot order for every SKU
- [ ] No phone numbers, email addresses, URLs or watermarks in the picture unless the platform allows it
- [ ] File names say what the image is, for example `ss-teapot-1l-brushed-side.jpg`
- [ ] One large master kept; platform copies exported from it
- [ ] Colour matches the real item (white balance fixed, not filtered)
- [ ] Mock-ups labelled
- [ ] Platform limits re-read on the live upload screen this month
