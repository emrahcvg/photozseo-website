**A wholesaler with thousands of SKUs does not need better photos; it needs one photo standard applied to every SKU, one key that joins images to the price list, and a quote template that looks like procurement paperwork.** Do those three things and the catalog scales to marketplaces, buyer portals and email attachments without redoing work.

*Last verified: November 2026. This guide describes workflow; it quotes no marketplace pixel values, so check each platform's current image guidelines (for example in its seller help center) before you upload.*

## What does a wholesaler's catalog problem look like?

Consider a hypothetical distributor of 2,000 SKUs in home and kitchen goods. About 600 images came from supplier PDFs, 700 from supplier ZIP files in different sizes, 300 were shot years ago by the sales team, and the rest do not exist. The price list lives in an Excel sheet where the SKU column has three different formats. A buyer asks for "your catalog with pictures," and someone spends two days stitching a PDF.

The cost is rarely the photos themselves. It is the inconsistency: nobody can say which image is current, which is licensed, and which matches which price line.

## Which image standard should a wholesaler write down?

One page, signed off once, applied to everything. A B2B buyer scanning 200 products wants to compare, so uniformity beats artistry.

| Rule | Example standard | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Canvas | One square size for the master (for example 2000 px), exported smaller per channel | Makes batch resizing predictable |
| Background | Plain white or a single neutral color | Products compare side by side |
| Product fill | Product occupies most of the frame, same margin everywhere | Avoids tiny items in big canvases |
| Angle order | 1 front, 2 back or side, 3 detail, 4 scale or pack | Same slot always means the same view |
| Pack shot | One image of the outer carton with the barcode readable | Buyers check case and unit |
| File name | SKU first: HK-10442-01.jpg | Joins photo to price list |
| Format | JPEG or marketplace-required format, sRGB | Predictable output |

Pick the numbers that match your most demanding channel, then derive the rest by resizing. Never shoot to the smallest requirement.

## How do you triage supplier images?

Do not rebuild the library; sort it.

| Bucket | Signs | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Usable as is | Clean background, sharp, correct product, permission clear | Rename, resize per channel |
| Fixable in bulk | Wrong size, uneven background, cluttered cropping, big file size | Batch crop, resize, compress, rename |
| Must reshoot | Watermark of another seller, wrong variant, low resolution, unclear license | Shoot yourself, or request a licensed pack |

Two cautions. First, ask about rights: supplier photos generally belong to the supplier or brand, and permission to use them in a PDF is not automatically permission to use them on a marketplace. Second, remove other sellers' logos or watermarks only when the rights are clear, otherwise reshoot. Start with your top 20 percent of SKUs by revenue; those images affect the most sales.

## How do you keep a price list and the photo library in sync?

Use the SKU as the only key. The photo file name starts with the SKU; the price list has a column for the SKU and one for the image file or URL. Any new SKU enters through that one table.

A B2B price list usually carries:

- SKU, barcode, product name and short description
- Unit of measure, pack size and minimum order quantity
- Price tiers by quantity, currency and VAT treatment
- Validity date and thumbnail
- HS code for cross-border buyers

## Price list or quote: which one do you send?

A price list is general; a quote is specific. When a buyer sends a request for 500 units to one address, the professional reply is a quote. Typical fields: quote number, validity date, both parties' legal details, line items with VAT and units, totals, currency, Incoterms for shipped orders, payment terms and bank details, and a signature.

## How should a wholesaler move onto a marketplace?

Do it in waves.

1. **Wave one: your best 50 to 100 SKUs.** Rebuild images to the marketplace's published rules; check the live guidelines rather than relying on old numbers.
2. **Fix identifiers.** SKU, barcode and brand must match your master sheet.
3. **Export in the platform's file format** and upload in a small batch to see which fields get rejected.
4. **Name files with SEO in mind** for public listings. Keep your internal SKU-based master untouched, and make marketplace copies in a separate folder.
5. **Wave two onward:** add the long tail once the first batch is clean.

A wholesaler selling in bulk on a B2B marketplace should also check whether the platform wants pack, carton or case photos and what the minimum order is.

## Team workflow for 2,000 SKUs

| Step | Who | Output |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Write standard | Catalog manager | One-page image standard |
| 2. Triage | Assistant | Bucket per SKU in the sheet |
| 3. Batch fix | Assistant | Renamed, resized images |
| 4. Reshoot queue | Warehouse + phone | Photos in standard setup, by revenue rank |
| 5. Review | Catalog manager | Spot check 5 percent of SKUs |
| 6. Publish | Sales ops | Price list, catalog PDF, channel exports |

## Pre-send checklist

- [ ] Every SKU has a main image and the same angle order.
- [ ] Image file names begin with the SKU.
- [ ] Licenses and permissions are recorded for supplier images.
- [ ] Price list columns include unit, MOQ, currency and VAT treatment.
- [ ] Quotes are numbered and have a validity date.
- [ ] Marketplace images follow the current rules of that marketplace.
