**Yes, a manufacturer can photograph its own products and sell them directly on its own website and on marketplaces, as long as it keeps a consistent shot set, one clean product record per item and the trade data an export buyer needs.** What decides success is not the camera. It is whether a buyer in another country can see the same product, in the same style, with measurements they trust and text in their language.

*Last verified: November 2026. Sources checked: World Customs Organization (Harmonized System compendium), ICC (Incoterms 2020 rules page), U.S. Copyright Office (Copyright FAQ). Marketplace rules change, so check each seller portal.*

## Why manufacturers photograph in-house

Imagine, for example, a small factory with 60 products in four colours that has always sold to wholesalers on the strength of a printed catalogue. Now it wants to sell direct: its own web shop, a Turkish marketplace such as Trendyol or Hepsiburada, and an international channel such as Amazon, eBay, Etsy or a B2B platform. Hiring a studio for 240 colour variants every season is expensive and slow, and the product mix changes. An in-house routine pays for itself fast, if it is boring and repeatable.

The advantage a manufacturer has over a reseller is access. You can shoot the product before it is boxed, shoot the real material and the real finish, and photograph the cross-section or the measuring detail. Reseller photos are often copied from you; yours are first-hand.

## The minimum shot set

Decide the list once and shoot every product against it.

| Shot | What it shows | Used for |
|---|---|---|
| Hero | Product centred on a plain background | Marketplace main image, web shop listing |
| Angle 2 and 3 | Side and back or top | Gallery |
| Detail | Material, stitching, joint, finish | Gallery, quality proof |
| Scale | Product next to a hand, a ruler or a familiar object | Sizing questions |
| In use or context | The product doing its job | Lifestyle gallery, ads |
| Packaging | What arrives at the buyer's door | Reduces "not as described" returns |

Marketplaces differ on what is allowed for the main picture, and many ask for a plain or white background. Check the current guideline of the platform before you shoot hero images. For a reference to platform sizes, see [marketplace image sizes](/marketplace-image-sizes); for the Turkish side, [Trendyol and Hepsiburada product photos](/blog/trendyol-hepsiburada-product-photos) explains why you should read each seller panel rather than trust a blog.

## Keep the set-up fixed

Consistency across 60 products is more important than any one beautiful picture.

- **One background and one light.** Even daylight from a window with a white reflector, or two continuous lights. Mark the tripod and product positions with tape.
- **One angle set** repeated in the same order, so a shopper comparing two products sees like with like.
- **A grey card or a colour reference** in a test shot every session. Colour accuracy matters to export buyers who cannot touch the product.
- **Colour variants in identical position.** Swap the product, not the camera. Name the files with the SKU and a colour code (for example, ABC-100_front_navy.jpg).

An iPhone on a tripod is enough for catalogue work; our guide on [product photography with an iPhone](/blog/product-photography-with-iphone-for-ecommerce-2026) covers the settings.

## One product record per item

The photos are half the job. The other half is the data a buyer, a marketplace and a customs officer each need. Keep one sheet, one row per SKU, and feed every channel from it.

| Field | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| SKU, barcode | Identifies the item on every channel |
| Name and short description (base language) | Source for translations |
| Material, dimensions, weight | The details export buyers filter by |
| Colour and size options | Variant groups on marketplaces and shop |
| Image file names (main + gallery) | Maps photos to the row |
| HS code and country of origin | Trade quotes and customs paperwork |
| Packaging dimensions and weight | Shipping and freight quotes |
| Certificates or standards (if any) | Some channels and buyers ask for them |

Marketplaces accept bulk uploads through spreadsheets. The article on [bulk CSV and XLSX import to 46 marketplaces](/blog/bulk-csv-xlsx-import-46-marketplaces-2026) explains how one sheet can feed several channels.

## HS codes without the guesswork

The Harmonized System is the product classification maintained by the World Customs Organization. According to the WCO's HS compendium, the system was designed as a core framework that countries add their own subdivisions to: the first six digits are the shared international code and longer codes are national extensions. The EU and the US, for example, use more digits than six.

What this means in practice:

1. Ask your customs broker or the customs authority in your country for the right code for each product family. A wrong code can delay shipments or change duty, and classification can depend on materials and use.
2. Store the confirmed code in your product sheet once, not in each quote.
3. Add the code to your B2B quotes. Buyers' procurement teams often ask for it.
4. Re-check when you change material or design.

This article does not give legal or tax advice, and tariff, VAT and export rules differ by country and change.

## Multilingual product text

Translate from a clean base description. Keep the structure the same in every language: name, three to five key benefits, then measurements and materials. Details that need care:

- **Units and sizes.** Provide metric and, if you target the US, imperial. Clothing and shoe sizes are not equivalent across markets.
- **Standards and certificates.** Use the official names; do not translate them by feel.
- **Search terms.** The words buyers use in Turkish, German or Spanish are rarely a literal translation. Look at how local marketplaces name similar products.
- **Alt text and file names** can be written per language; see [alt text examples](/blog/product-image-alt-text-examples).

## Choosing channels

| Channel type | Examples | Typical approach |
|---|---|---|
| Own web shop | Shopify, WooCommerce | Full control of images and text; you handle traffic and payments |
| Home marketplaces | Trendyol, Hepsiburada | Large local reach; follow seller panel rules |
| International marketplaces | Amazon, eBay, Etsy | Reach plus each platform's rules and fees |
| B2B and sourcing platforms | Alibaba, Made-in-China, Global Sources | Quote-based; buyers want specs, MOQ and certificates |

For a B2B route, quote terms matter: the ICC lists eleven Incoterms 2020 rules for allocating cost and risk between buyer and seller, so choose one deliberately and name the place, for instance "FOB" plus a named port. [The B2B quote builder guide](/blog/b2b-quote-builder-2026) covers the fields in a quote.

Be careful with images from your own contract photographers and with images of components licensed by someone else. The U.S. Copyright Office says photographs are protected automatically; make sure you hold the rights to every picture you publish.

## A sample two-week launch plan

1. Days 1 to 2: build the product sheet for the 10 best-selling items.
2. Days 3 to 5: shoot those 10 with the fixed set-up; rename with the SKU rule.
3. Days 6 to 8: prepare channel versions (sizes, compression) for the web shop and one marketplace.
4. Days 9 to 10: write base text and translate; have a native speaker check it.
5. Days 11 to 12: upload, review listing previews, correct what the marketplace flags.
6. Days 13 to 14: look at which listings got views, then expand to the rest of the range.

## Checklist

- [ ] Shot list, background and light fixed and documented.
- [ ] Every SKU photographed with the same angles and colour variants in the same position.
- [ ] HS code confirmed by a customs professional and stored per product family.
- [ ] Measurements checked against the physical product.
- [ ] Base text translated and reviewed by a native speaker for each target market.
- [ ] Each marketplace's current guidelines read before the first upload.
