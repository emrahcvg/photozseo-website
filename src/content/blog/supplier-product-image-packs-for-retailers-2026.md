**A supplier image pack is a downloadable set of product photos, organised by SKU, with a data file that says which photo belongs to which product and a short note on how resellers may use them.** Retailers who can load your pack straight into a shop or marketplace list your products faster, make fewer mistakes and are more likely to reorder. This guide walks through a practical pack structure, a naming rule, size tiers, the usage note and the CSV that holds it together.

*Last verified: November 2026. Sources checked: U.S. Copyright Office (Copyright FAQ), Shopify Help Center (Using CSV files to import and export products), ICC (Incoterms 2020 rules page).*

## Why retailers ask for an image pack

Picture a supplier with, for example, 300 SKUs of kitchenware. Twenty retailers each send the same emails every season: "Can you send photos of the new colours?" "Which file is the 24 cm pan?" "Is the lid in the picture included?" The supplier answers with a shared drive link, file names like IMG_4821.jpg, and a price list in a different order from the folders.

The retailer then spends hours matching photos to rows by hand. Mistakes are almost guaranteed: the wrong colour on the wrong listing, a set photographed with accessories that the SKU does not include, and the same photo uploaded under two products. A pack removes that work and moves it to the one party that knows the product best, the supplier.

## What goes in the pack

| Item | What it is | Why retailers need it |
|---|---|---|
| Photos by SKU | One folder per SKU, or one flat folder with the SKU in every file name | Quick import and no guessing |
| Product data CSV | One row per SKU: name, barcode, colour, size, and the image file names | Maps photos to products and to the price list |
| Usage-rights note | One page: what resellers may and may not do | Removes the permission question |
| Readme | The naming rule, the size tiers, the pack date | Lets a new employee use the pack alone |
| Change log | What was added or replaced since the last pack | Retailers update only what changed |

Keep the first version small. A pack that covers your best 50 SKUs and is accurate is more useful than a complete archive that is half out of date.

## A naming rule retailers can sort and search

Use one pattern for every file and write it in the readme. A simple, readable form is the SKU, then a view code, then an optional colour code:

- SKU-12345_front.jpg
- SKU-12345_side.jpg
- SKU-12345_back-red.jpg
- SKU-12345_detail-handle.jpg

Rules worth keeping: lowercase letters, digits, hyphens and underscores only; no spaces or accented characters, because some upload systems and operating systems handle them badly; no "final", "new" or "copy" in names. Because the SKU leads the name, files sort next to each other and can be matched to a price list with a simple lookup.

For search engines, descriptive file names help, but a supplier pack is a source file. Many retailers will rename to their own keyword-first names when they publish. Our guide on [how to name product photos for SEO](/blog/how-to-name-product-photos-for-seo) shows what that second step looks like, so you know why a clean SKU-based original is the best starting point.

## Size tiers: master, web and thumbnail

Marketplaces change their image rules, so do not try to pre-fit one file to every platform. Instead give each photo a few tiers and tell retailers where they came from. The numbers below are a suggested starting set, not a platform requirement.

| Tier | Suggested use | Suggested spec (supplier's choice) |
|---|---|---|
| Master | Archive and re-cropping | Original or high resolution JPEG or PNG, no heavy compression |
| Web | Shop and most marketplaces | Square canvas, long edge around 2000 px, JPEG |
| Thumbnail | Lists, quotes, catalogues | About 600 px on the long edge |

If you know your retailers sell on a specific platform, add a folder sized for it. Check each platform's current seller guidelines first. For a reference to what several marketplaces expect, see [marketplace image sizes](/marketplace-image-sizes). Pixel values change, so the page links to where they come from instead of promising they are permanent.

## Photo consistency matters more than resolution

A pack photographed on different days with different light looks bad on a retailer's page, even if every file is sharp. Before shooting, fix three things: the background (white or light grey, the same for the whole range), the camera angle set (for example front, side, back, detail, scale) and the framing, so the product fills a similar share of the frame. Use a tripod or a fixed distance. A fixed set of angles also keeps retailers from asking you for "one more picture".

For variants, shoot every colour in the same position and name them with a colour code. Do not photograph one colour and tell retailers to change it in an editor.

## The usage-rights note

Photographs are protected by copyright the moment they are created. The U.S. Copyright Office FAQ says a work is under protection once it is created and fixed in a tangible form, and that registration is voluntary. That means a retailer cannot safely assume your files are free for them to use, and a good retailer will ask. A short note answers it in advance.

What the note usually covers, written in your own words and checked by a lawyer in your country:

1. Who owns the photos (you, or a photographer with a licence to you).
2. Who may use them (your authorised resellers, as long as they stock your products).
3. Where (their own shop, marketplaces, social posts, printed catalogues).
4. What they may change (resize, crop and rename, but not add misleading text or logos of other brands).
5. What happens when the relationship ends (remove the images within a reasonable period).

This is a communication tool, not legal advice. If your photos include a photographer's work, models or someone else's brand, confirm your own rights first.

## The CSV that connects everything

The CSV is the part retailers value most. One row per SKU, with columns in a fixed order. A practical minimum:

| Column | Example |
|---|---|
| sku | SKU-12345 |
| barcode | the product's EAN or UPC, if it has one |
| product_name | the name you want used |
| colour, size | variant fields |
| image_main | SKU-12345_front.jpg |
| image_2 to image_6 | other view files |
| pack_version | 2026-11 |

If your retailers use Shopify, this maps naturally onto its product CSV. According to the Shopify Help Center, the product CSV has columns for the product image URL, the image position, the image alt text and a separate variant image URL, and Shopify downloads the images from public URLs during import and re-uploads them to the store. The alt text field can hold up to 512 characters, and Shopify recommends keeping it to 125 or fewer. Upload your web tier to a public address, add the URLs to the CSV and a retailer can import the entire range in one go. Read Shopify's current page before promising any column name to a partner. The comparison of import methods in [Shopify CSV vs API](/blog/shopify-csv-vs-api-2026) helps you decide which route to document for them.

If you also provide wholesale terms, include the HS code and country of origin in a separate trade-data tab. Your retailer or customs broker uses these for cross-border orders, and you should confirm the correct classification with a qualified professional.

## A worked example workflow

An illustrative weekly routine for the 300-SKU supplier above:

1. Shoot new or changed products on the fixed set-up.
2. Rename the originals with the SKU rule.
3. Export the three tiers into the pack folders.
4. Update the CSV rows and the pack version.
5. Test: open the CSV, take five random rows and check that each file name exists in the folders.
6. Publish the pack to one shared link and email retailers a two-line change log.

Step five is the one most suppliers skip, and it catches nearly all errors.

## Checklist before you send a pack

- [ ] Every SKU in the price list has at least one photo in the pack.
- [ ] Every file name follows the rule, with no spaces.
- [ ] Every file name in the CSV exists, and the reverse.
- [ ] The background and angle set are consistent across the range.
- [ ] No third-party logos, watermarks or unlicensed models are visible.
- [ ] The usage-rights note and the version date are in the pack.
- [ ] A one-line contact is included for retailers who need a missing photo.
